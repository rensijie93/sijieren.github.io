About

I am a historian of science, technology, and medicine in modern China, with a particular interest in how scientific work becomes entangled with political legitimacy, mass mobilization, and everyday moral life under socialism. My research moves between the laboratory bench, the archive, and the self-criticism session — reading scientific practice as a form of political and social history.

I completed my PhD at the University of Bristol under the supervision of Robert Bickers, with Rana Mitter serving as external examiner. I am currently based at Peking University, supervised by Zhang Li (张藜) as part of the Boya Postdoctoral Fellowship.

Research

Dissertation

China's Synthetic Insulin Project, 1958–1965

A history of the world's first total synthesis of a protein, read as a case study in scientific mobilization, international competition, and the politics of credit in the early PRC. Winner of the 2025 BACS Best Doctoral Thesis Prize.

In progress

The Moral Economy of Toil (辛劳的道德经济)

A new project tracing "辛苦" (toil, hardship) as a form of moral currency across commendation reports, self-criticism documents, and model-worker narratives in PRC political discourse.

Working paper

Self-Criticism Documents as Sources for Cold War Knowledge Transfer

Centered on the marine biologists Zeng Chengkui (C. K. Tseng) and Zhang Xi at the CAS Institute of Oceanology.

Book
